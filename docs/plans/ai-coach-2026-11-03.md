# AI coach: plan for review (3 Nov 2026)

A coach you can ask questions about your own games: "Why is Qh6 bad?", "What was I missing here?",
"What if I'd played Nf3?", "What should I work on?". It uses an open-weight model on a free hosted
service. The model only does the talking. Every chess fact comes from the engine and the app's own
analysis.

## 1. Cost: nothing

| | Free allowance | Card needed | Over the limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Groq (default)**, model `gpt-oss-120b` | ~1,000 requests and ~200,000 tokens a day, 8,000 tokens a minute | No, according to most sources (to be checked when signing up) | Requests are refused until the next day. You are not charged. |
| OpenRouter, `:free` models (backup) | 50 requests a day | No | Refused (error 429) |

- You create a free Groq account and paste its key into Settings. Nothing is paid, now or later.
  If Groq asks for a card at sign-up, we stop there and use OpenRouter's free models instead.
- The app keeps its own daily budget below the free limits, so it never gets to the point of being
  refused. It shows "18 questions left today".
- Answers are saved on the phone. Opening the same question again costs nothing.
- Hosting stays on GitHub Pages (free). There is no server: I checked that both Groq and OpenRouter
  accept requests straight from the browser.

**Rough capacity:** a typical question is one request of about 3,000 tokens. That is about 50–60
questions a day and 2–3 a minute. "What if…" questions need 2–3 requests.

## 2. How it avoids talking nonsense

Language models on their own get chess wrong. In a 2026 study, Qwen3-8B got 55% of its claims wrong,
and even GPT-5.4 got 22% wrong. With engine tools, the errors fell by half or more. So the setup is:

1. **Facts first.** Every question is sent together with what the app already knows about that move:
   - the classification and evaluation before and after;
   - the mistake type and the coach's explanation;
   - the best line and the line that punishes your move;
   - the material count and pieces that are hanging or under attack (from chess.js).
2. **Tools for anything else.** The model can ask for:
   - `analyse(position)`: Stockfish's top three lines, from the engine the app already runs;
   - `play(position, moves)`: is the line legal, and what is the position and evaluation after it;
   - `attackers(square)`: what attacks and defends a square;
   - `my_profile()`: your mistake types, phases, focus area and openings, from Insights.
3. **Rules in the prompt.** Never name a move or an evaluation that didn't come from the facts or a
   tool. Say "I'm not sure" rather than guess. Write short, plain answers.
4. **Checking after.** Every move the answer mentions is checked to be legal and to appear in the
   facts or tool results. An answer that fails is regenerated once; if it fails again, the bad parts
   are cut and marked. Moves in the answer become tappable and open on the board.
5. **Respects "Best moves" off.** The best move is left out of the facts, and the coach gives hints
   rather than the answer ("Look at the e-file") unless you ask for it.

## 3. What you get, and where

**A. Ask about a move (review)**
- An "Ask" button on the coach card opens a chat about the current move.
- Suggested questions to start with:
  - "Why is this bad?"
  - "Give me a hint"
  - "What was the idea behind the best move?"
  - "What should I have been thinking about?"
- Or type your own question. You can also play a move on the board and ask "what about this?".

**B. Puzzles.** "Why?" after you solve or miss a puzzle explains the idea, not just the line.

**C. Personal feedback (Insights → Skills)**
- "Coach's view" is a short written assessment of your last 20–30 games, built from the numbers in
  Insights (mistake types, phases, thrown games, time). It is updated on request, at most once a
  day.
- Examples: "Most of your points go in the middlegame when your opponent has a knight jump you
  didn't see…", followed by 2–3 concrete things to practise, with links to puzzles of that type.

**D. (Later, if A–C work well) Free questions about your games**, for example "Which openings
cost me the most this month?". These are answered from the database.

## 4. Settings, key and privacy

- **Settings → AI coach:** off by default. It has:
  - a field for the key, a "Test" button, and the questions left today;
  - an "Answers in" choice: English or Swedish.
- **The key:**
  - It is stored only on the device, in the app's own storage, and is left out of backups.
  - It never goes to GitHub.
  - Each device needs the key pasted once.
- **What is sent:** positions, moves, evaluations and your statistics. Your username and opponents'
  names are not sent. Groq keeps request logs for up to 30 days. A "Zero data retention" switch in
  Groq's settings turns this off, and I'll show you where it is. Groq doesn't train on API data,
  according to its terms (I've only seen this quoted, not on Groq's own page).
- **Offline:** the Ask button is hidden, and saved answers can still be read.

## 5. Order of work, with checkpoints for you

0. **Trial before building anything (about half a day).**
   - You create the Groq account; I check that it is free with no card.
   - Test on 30 positions from your own games, compared side by side:
     - `gpt-oss-120b` with and without facts;
     - Groq's alternative model (`qwen3.8-27b`).
   - I check every claim in the answers against Stockfish and report the error rate, with example
     answers.
   - **You decide go or no-go.** If it's wrong too often, we stop here.
1. **The facts layer and tools:** pure functions with unit tests, no AI involved.
2. **Ask about a move (A)** in the review, with the checking step, the saved answers and the daily
   budget.
3. **Puzzles "Why?" (B)** and **Coach's view (C)**.
4. **Tests:** browser tests with a fake AI service (like the fake chess.com service), and unit tests
   for the checking step. The real service is never called in CI.

## 6. Risks

- **Free terms can change.** The feature switches itself off with a clear message, and the backup
  service is ready.
- **Still sometimes wrong.** The checking step catches wrong or illegal moves, but not a bad plan in
  words. Answers carry a small "AI, may be wrong" label.
- **8,000 tokens a minute is tight.** We keep the facts compact and send one request where possible.
  If you ask questions in quick succession, there may be a short wait ("Wait 20 s").

## 7. Decisions for you

1. Go ahead with step 0 (the trial), and create a free Groq account when I ask?
2. Which features: A, B, C (recommended: all three, A first), and D later?
3. Should answers be in English, Swedish, or your choice in Settings?
4. With "Best moves" off, should the coach give hints only, or reveal the best move when you ask?
